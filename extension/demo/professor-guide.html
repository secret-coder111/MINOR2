Five-minute walkthrough

A sequence you can explain

  1. Ordinary login. Open the baseline, inspect its origin and relative form destination, and read the live extension result.

  2. External destination. Show the same visitor-to-recipient animation. Explain that an unauthorized recipient could receive a submission, while a legitimate identity provider may also be external.

  3. Combined evidence. Show the brand/address conflict and external credential destination. Read the actual warning reasons. Play the fictional collector view.

  4. Let the examiner change it. Choose the order, edit a harmless form action or title in DevTools, and use Check again. Restore the baseline and show the warning clear.

  5. Use independent tests. Compare an examiner-chosen legitimate page and the official fixtures. Record a miss or false warning honestly.

What each check establishes

Three kinds of evidence

Controlled mechanism test

Changing one input isolates why a rule changes. Hosting the same fixture online does not turn it into an independent accuracy benchmark.

Independent public fixture

BadSSL tests a real browser's handling of insecure transport. AMTSO and Google's pages test participating browser/vendor protection. Those are not live criminal sites.

Independent performance evaluation

A reviewed, unseen, source/time-separated dataset is needed to estimate missed phishing and false warnings. This classroom demonstration does not supply an accuracy percentage.

Open the independent tests

“How do we know the demo is not faking the answer?”

Expand the page facts: the title, browser origin and resolved form action come from the document. Inspect the form in browser DevTools and change it yourself. Read the installed extension's independent toolbar popup. The page's scenario label and animation do not feed a result into the extension.

The teaching JavaScript reads no input values. The fields are read-only; the HTML Content Security Policy blocks form submission and network connections from page scripts. The local demo server also rejects POST requests. Inspect the Network panel: advancing the animation generates no credential request.

These controls establish this mechanism and this implementation's behavior. They do not establish protection against every possible phishing technique.

Questions you should be ready to answer

Does every external destination mean phishing?

No. Authorized identity/payment services and SSO can cross site boundaries. The recipient's authorization and independent evidence matter.

Does HTTPS make a fake page safe?

No. It protects transport to the domain you reached. A deceptive operator can have HTTPS too.

Does this demonstration actually steal a password?

No. The comparison pages use read-only fields and fixed visual tokens. Native submissions and page-script connections are blocked. The URL checker accepts only a website address and does not visit it.

How does the AMTSO test differ from the rule score?

Updated PhishGuard recognises the exact official AMTSO phishing-test address and displays a harmless security-test warning. This built-in test entry leaves the structural rule score unchanged. Google's separate test is not included in that entry. Other products may block first; attribute their interstitials to them. Recognition of a known test URL does not establish general detection accuracy.

What remains outside this extension's coverage?

Unknown brand ownership, visual-only deception, arbitrary script data flow, closed/opaque content and unmeasured generalisation remain limitations. Low risk does not verify a site's owner.
